# Location catalog

268 bundled destinations: the original set, 42 additions across the eight mapped regions, 20 boss arenas (La Signora’s since catalog 17), the 80 catalog-14 places and a Statue of The Seven in every region (catalog 21; see the last three sections). Existing IDs and user artwork are preserved; verified coordinate corrections migrate only untouched bundled positions. Character defaults follow the five-resident cap. Meeting points do not imply permanent canonical homes.

Images were visually reviewed for playable characters, game HUD, captions and aerial viewpoints. Ordinary NPCs and official logos may remain. Some backgrounds show an approach or adjacent scenery, disclosed below. Svetloledovka retains its earlier disclosed AI-cleaned screenshot. Distances are straight-line map estimates, not canonical travel times. Surface-map coordinates cannot represent vertical floors.

## Boss arenas (catalog version 11)

Each of the 19 Genius Invokation TCG boss cards has its own Wilderness waypoint at the HoYoLAB marker for its arena or trounce-domain entrance. Coordinates are copied from the marker without conversion. Every arena has a fixed 100% encounter chance and the boss as its only enemy, so each arrival starts that fight (arrivals by opening a chat do not). Backgrounds come from the Genshin Impact Wiki API at their original dimensions: domain entrances and a few arenas show the real spot, while others show the nearest named subarea as disclosed. Combat music is the boss or region combat theme named by the wiki's Soundtrack Usage, downloaded with the earlier music importer. Exploration music reuses the nearest documented waypoint playlist.

## Map expansion (catalog version 14, 6 November 2026)

Ten more destinations per region, 80 in all, chosen from the HoYoLAB map for lore, story and character relevance, with priority to named areas farthest from any existing waypoint (most new pins are 300 to 3,000 map units from the nearest older one). Each pin copies a HoYoLAB viewpoint marker or the centre of an official map label, without conversion; the entry says which. Names, descriptions and enemies come from the Genshin Impact Wiki API. Novokitezhgrad and Wingspan Peak (eastern Snezhnaya) are drawn only on the current map art, so the bundled map was updated to HoYoLAB version `bd70cde160dbd523926208d9fa8961a1` at the same time (see [MAP-RESEARCH.md](../research/MAP-RESEARCH.md)).

Backgrounds were reviewed on contact sheets against the [asset guide](../development/ASSET-GUIDELINES.md). Most are the wiki's in-game image of the place. Where that image was aerial, or the only HoYoLAB viewpoint photo showed a character or the HUD, a ground-level HoYoLAB photo of the nearest Teleport Waypoint is used instead; the waypoint stands in the scene, and the entry says so. Natural landscapes seen from a cliff edge were kept; settlements seen from above were not. Images wider than 2,560 px were downscaled; all were recompressed to WebP. Ordinary NPCs and the game logo may remain.

Music follows the wiki's Soundtrack Usage for each place, day and night. When no track is documented for the place itself, the containing area's ambience or a nearby waypoint's playlist is used, and the entry says so. The wiki documents no soundtrack for Novokitezhgrad or Wingspan Peak yet, so those use Snezhnaya tracks already in the library. The wiki hosts none of these tracks as audio, so the 111 new files were downloaded from the [kuutar OST library](https://music.kuutar.moe) with the same verified method as earlier rounds; their hashes and sources are in [the catalog 14 music manifest](../research/music/2026-11-06/map-music.json). Wilderness waypoints take their region's combat track.

Forty-nine places are Wilderness. Their pools favour enemies the wiki lists for the place, then enemies that appear in the fewest other pools of the region; bosses are left to the boss arenas. Places with no wiki enemy list get region-native enemies that suit the terrain. No residents are assigned.

## Statues of The Seven (catalog version 21, 11 November 2026)

One Statue of The Seven per region, each copied from a marker on HoYoLAB's Statue of The Seven layer (label 2) without conversion. Nod-Krai has none; the same layer marks its Statues of the New Moon, so Hiisi Island's stands in. A statue waypoint carries `statue` with its element (`New Moon` in Nod-Krai) and is drawn with the statue's in-game map icon ([`UI_Statue_of_The_Seven.png`](https://genshin-impact.fandom.com/wiki/File:UI_Statue_of_The_Seven.png) from the Genshin Impact Wiki API) instead of the waypoint pin.

Arriving at a statue with Aether or Lumine in the party, or while playing one of them, offers to change their element to the statue's, once per arrival; the statue's map card offers it again while the party is there. The Statue of the New Moon grants no element, as in the game.

Arriving at any statue restores the party's HP, even if it is set as wilderness; its map card can restore it again while the party is there. Catalog version 23 makes **Starfell Lake** a Statue of The Seven (Anemo) for its island statue, in addition to Windwail Highland. Players can make any waypoint a statue, or stop it being one, with the Statue field in the waypoint editor.

Seven Statue of The Seven backgrounds were replaced in catalog version 22 with user-approved AI-generated landscape interpretations (1672×941), generated from the regional statue references and location research; Windwail Highland also uses user-provided screenshots. The [art run](../../art/location-backgrounds/2026-11-11-seven-statues/manifest.json) records prompts and references. Nod-Krai retains the non-generative composite of its official portrait render. The linked image sources below are statue reference images, not the generated backgrounds. Each statue shares the music of the nearest waypoint in its region that has a playlist. No residents, no wilderness.
